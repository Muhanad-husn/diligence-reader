Cyber and Privacy Liability Insurance — Policy Schedule and Key Terms. This schedule summarises the
principal terms of the Company's cyber tower for the 2025/26 policy year. It is a summary for diligence purposes;
the bound wordings and endorsements govern in the event of any conflict.

2. Insuring agreements (summary)

• Privacy and network-security liability, including unauthorised access to or acquisition of personal data.
• Regulatory defence and penalties arising from a covered privacy event, subject to the sublimit above and to
insurability at law.

• Breach-response costs, including notification, credit monitoring and public-relations costs through the insurer's
panel.

• Cyber business interruption and digital asset restoration.

3. Conditions precedent and notice

Notice of circumstances (Condition 7.2). The insured shall give written notice to the insurer, through the broker,
as soon as practicable and in any event within 45 days of any director or officer, the General Counsel, the Chief
Information Security Officer or the Director of Treasury & Insurance becoming aware of any circumstance
reasonably likely to give rise to a claim under this policy. Failure to give notice within the 45-day period may entitle
the insurer to decline cover for the relevant claim to the extent it is prejudiced.

Awareness. For the purposes of Condition 7.2, awareness arises when a relevant officer first knows, or ought
reasonably to know, of facts indicating that a claim may result — and is not deferred until the insured has
completed its own investigation or received a final report.

4. Principal exclusions

• Prior known events. The policy does not cover any claim arising out of, based upon or attributable to any act,
error, circumstance, incident or event of which any relevant officer was aware, or any notice of which had been
given under any prior policy, before the inception of this policy year; nor any matter that, as at inception, a
reasonable person in the insured's position would have expected to give rise to a claim.
• Bodily injury and property damage (save as expressly extended).
• Prior and pending matters scheduled as known at inception.
• Fines and penalties to the extent uninsurable as a matter of applicable law.

• Failure to maintain agreed minimum security controls, including failure to rotate cryptographic keys and to
retire end-of-life systems in accordance with the insured's own policies, where such failure materially
contributes to the loss.
