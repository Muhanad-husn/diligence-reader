Re Contingency reserve — Trust Reset / trust & safety matters (FY2025)

1. Purpose

This memorandum sets out the basis for the contingency reserve recognised in the draft FY2025 financial
statements in respect of trust-and-safety matters, including the operational account-integrity programme (the Trust
Reset programme), and explains why management recommends a reserve of $12m at this time.

2. Recommendation

Management recommends recognising a trust-and-safety contingency of $12m within other liabilities and
provisions at 31 December 2025. This amount represents management's best estimate of the obligation that is
both probable and reliably measurable at the reporting date, principally comprising committed remediation and
professional-fee costs and identifiable customer-support and programme costs.

3. Basis for the estimate

• Under the applicable recognition criteria, a provision is recognised only where a present obligation is probable
and the amount can be reliably estimated.

• The committed and reasonably foreseeable costs of the operational programme and related professional fees
are estimable and are reflected in the $12m amount.

• Management has considered, but does not at this time recognise, amounts in respect of matters that are not,
in its assessment, sufficiently certain or measurable.

4. Matters not provided for

Management is aware that a wider range of possible outcomes could arise depending on the conclusions of an
ongoing operational review and any subsequent assessment of notification or regulatory obligations. In the
absence of a confirmed reportable event, and given that the outcome of the review is not currently estimable
with sufficient reliability, management does not consider it appropriate to recognise a further provision at this time.
The position will be reassessed as the review progresses and as further information becomes available.

5. Acknowledged range

For completeness, management acknowledges that, were certain contingencies to crystallise, the eventual cost
could be materially higher than the amount recognised. Management does not, however, regard those
contingencies as currently meeting the threshold for recognition, and notes that outside counsel continues to
advise on the relevant thresholds. This memorandum should be read together with the legal exposure assessment
