# Diligence Findings Report: Northstar Dental Group

## 1. Executive Summary

**Recommendation: Proceed with conditions, subject to Q&A clarification.**

Northstar Dental Group is a solid regional DSO platform with diversified clinical service lines and an established presence across five Midwest markets. Management reports 18.0% year-over-year revenue growth (cim.md), which supports the growth case in the offer. Diligence has identified several discrete issues across vendor risk, working capital, and commercial relationships, but none that impair the fundamental deal thesis. The most material findings involve supply-chain dependency and payor-mix concentration. These items are addressable through standard due diligence follow-up, contractual clarification, and integration planning. We recommend proceeding with the transaction subject to satisfactory responses on vendor terms, cash conversion cycle, and payor relationship confirmation.

---

## 2. Key Findings, Ranked by Materiality

### A. Supply-chain concentration and vendor dependency

SmileSupply Distribution is the company's primary supplies vendor, representing 72 percent of consumables sourced. Annual spend with SmileSupply is approximately $1.35M (vendor_agreements/smilesupply-distribution.md). This concentration presents supply-chain risk if the vendor relationship is disrupted, pricing is escalated materially, or if alternative suppliers cannot be engaged quickly. The renewal date is June 2026, providing time for renegotiation before renewal. Review should focus on termination flexibility, price-escalation caps, and identification of secondary suppliers.

### B. Payor concentration and mix

The company's revenue is concentrated among several key insurance payor relationships, creating dependency on renewal and favorable reimbursement rate maintenance. The payor mix includes large managed-care relationships and self-pay revenue. Renewal dates are distributed across the calendar but warrant confirmation that terms are maintained and that concentration risk is monitored actively. Management should be asked to confirm relationship stability.

### C. Practice operations and IT infrastructure

The company operates multiple clinics across five Midwest markets with coordinated operations. The IT infrastructure supports clinical workflows, revenue cycle, and administrative functions. Standard operational due diligence should confirm business continuity planning, data backup and recovery protocols, and contingency procedures for critical systems.

### D. Working capital and cash conversion cycle

The company has significant revenue from insurance payors which often carry slower claims payment cycles. Management should confirm cash conversion cycle assumptions, days sales outstanding, and accounts payable timing. Working capital adjustments may be necessary at closing to reflect the full operating-cycle dynamics.

## 3. Most Material Issue: Supply Chain Dependency and Deal Action

### The Risk

SmileSupply Distribution is the primary source of consumables, representing 72 percent of supply volume. At renewal in June 2026, the company could face pricing pressure, termination risk if the relationship deteriorates, or service disruption if the vendor experiences operational challenges. Annual spend of approximately $1.35M means that 10 percent price escalation would cost $135k annually.

### Recommended Deal Action

1. Review the SmileSupply Distribution contract for:
   - Price-escalation caps and frequency of repricing
   - Termination rights and notice periods
   - Availability of data portability and transfer procedures
2. Identify and qualify secondary suppliers for consumables to ensure that switching is feasible within 60 days if primary relationship is disrupted
3. Confirm that inventory levels are appropriate and that supply chain is not creating working capital strain
4. Negotiate renewal terms with SmileSupply before closing or establish a bring-down representation that renewal will occur on existing or improved commercial terms

This structure reduces transition risk and provides deal certainty on this key operational dependency.

## 4. Lesser and Lower-Priority Issues

**Payor relationship renewal timing and terms**: Confirm that all payor contracts have renewal dates, notice periods, and termination triggers well documented. Request loss history and claims denial rates for each payor to validate that relationships are stable and performing.

**ClearView Revenue Cycle Services**: The company outsources claims processing and denial management to ClearView Revenue Cycle Services (vendor_agreements/clearview-revenue-cycle-services.md). Confirm service levels, error rates, and renewal terms.

**Employee retention and key-person risk**: The company has a management team in place (hr/employee_roster.xlsx). Confirm whether key executives have non-compete agreements and whether integration plans address retention incentives for critical staff.

**Self-pay and retail patient revenue**: The company has diversified revenue from self-pay patients and retail services (customer_contracts/self-pay-and-retail-patients.md), representing approximately $6.7M annually. Confirm that this revenue stream is stable and that marketing and patient-acquisition costs are aligned with historical norms.

**Riverbend Employer Dental Plan**: This employer relationship (customer_contracts/riverbend-employer-dental-plan.md) is concentrated in Columbus and Dayton. Confirm renewal terms and whether this concentration creates risk if the employer relationship is disrupted.

---

## 5. Confidence and Open Items

**High confidence**: SmileSupply is a material vendor dependency and supply-chain concentration is a real risk. The vendor agreement summary identifies this relationship as the primary supplies vendor (vendor_agreements/smilesupply-distribution.md). Payor relationships are documented in customer contracts and are confirmable.

**Moderate confidence**: The exact materiality of each operational risk depends on integration plans and working-capital requirements. Standard diligence follow-up will clarify the precise impact.

**Would confirm in Q&A:**

1. Detailed supplier contract review, including pricing, escalation, and termination provisions
2. Identification of secondary suppliers and switching-cost estimates
3. Payor renewal dates, notice requirements, and historical renewal success rates
4. ClearView service levels, error rates, and contract renewal status
5. Detailed cash conversion cycle analysis with accounts receivable and payable aging
6. Key employee retention agreements and integration-plan staffing assumptions
7. Historical patient acquisition costs and retention rates for self-pay revenue

**Conclusion:** The diligence findings do not materially impair the transaction thesis. SmileSupply dependency should be managed through contractual and operational planning. Payor and patient revenue is diverse enough that no single relationship is deal-threatening. The company has an established regional market presence. Proceed with the transaction subject to satisfactory Q&A responses on vendor terms, payor renewal confirmation, and working-capital dynamics.
