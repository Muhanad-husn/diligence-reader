The matter. Over the autumn the Company ran a staged account-security exercise, and separately a forensic review of some anomalous activity in
our October logs is ongoing under the workstream we have been calling the workstream. The two are
related operationally. I am genuinely unsure whether, for notice purposes, we describe the
circumstance as the workstream (the forensic review of the October activity) or simply as the
account-security programme. The label matters because it affects how the
carrier reads the trigger and the prior-known-events position.

The timing problem. Condition 7.2 requires notice within 45 days of a relevant officer
becoming aware of a circumstance likely to give rise to a claim. If "awareness" is taken
from when Security first opened the October ticket (around 21 October), the 45-day
window would have closed on or about 5 December 2025, and this draft is plainly later than that.
If instead awareness is measured from receipt of the final summary in late November, we are
arguably still inside a reasonable period. I do not want to overstate our position to the
carrier, but nor do I want to concede an early awareness date that hands them the
prior-known-events exclusion.

What I would like from you:

  1. Your view on whether to characterise the circumstance as the workstream or as the programme in the formal notice, and the coverage consequences of each.
  2. Whether we date awareness from the October ticket or from the final summary, and how
     to frame that defensibly without misstating anything.
  3. Whether a protective notice of circumstances now (caveated) is better than continuing
     to hold, given the 45-day condition and the prior-known-events exclusion.

I am conscious we are past the date that the more conservative reading of the 45-day
clock would imply, so I would rather move on this quickly than perfectly. Please do not
