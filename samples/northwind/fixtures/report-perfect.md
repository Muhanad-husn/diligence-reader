# Diligence Findings Report: Northwind Logistics Software

## 1. Executive Summary

**Recommendation: Reprice or restructure via special indemnity.**

Northwind Logistics Software presents a valuable SaaS platform anchored by strong customer relationships and recurring revenue. However, diligence has uncovered a material revenue-cliff exposure that must be addressed at close. Meridian Freight Corporation, representing 30.1% of ARR, holds a change-of-control termination right that is exercisable upon closing of the Summit acquisition. The Meridian MSA grants an automatic, customer-elected termination right effective immediately upon written notice delivered within sixty (60) days of change of control, with pro-rata refunds of any prepaid fees and no wind-down obligations.

The financial impact is material. Meridian accounts for $12,400,000 of the company's $41,200,000 total ARR, representing a direct revenue cliff at close. Beyond Meridian, secondary gaps include termination-for-convenience rights at Harbor Foods, a revenue-recognition uncertainty on the Cobalt Retail prepaid order, EU data-processing risks on the Tidewater engagement, and unresolved intellectual-property ownership of core platform algorithms built by an independent contractor.

The recommended deal action is to price the $12.4m cliff at close, through a price reduction, an escrow or a special indemnity of that amount, unless Meridian waives Section 12.3(c) or confirms in writing before close that it will not exercise it.

## 2. Key Findings, Ranked by Materiality

### A. Meridian Freight change-of-control revenue cliff

Meridian Freight Corporation is the company's largest customer, accounting for $12,400,000 of total ARR and 30.1% of annual recurring revenue (sample_data_room/Northwind_Logistics/arr_schedule.xlsx.md). The three-year Meridian MSA effective January 1, 2025, running through December 31, 2027, contains a change-of-control provision that presents material risk.

Section 12.3(c) of the Meridian MSA states verbatim: "Notwithstanding any other provision of this Agreement, including Sections 11.1 and 12.2, in the event of a Change of Control of Provider, Customer may terminate this Agreement, effective immediately, by delivering written notice of such election to Provider (or its successor) at any time within sixty (60) days following the later of (i) the closing of the Change of Control or (ii) Customer's receipt of the notice required under Section 12.3(b). Such termination shall be automatic and effective upon Customer's written election, and shall not require the consent of Provider or its successor, nor shall it be subject to any cure period, transition period, or wind-down obligation." (sample_data_room/Northwind_Logistics/msa_meridian_freight.pdf.md)

This represents an unconditioned, customer-elected termination right triggered by the proposed Summit acquisition. The provision is self-executing upon Meridian's written notice and requires no performance or dispute resolution before loss of the $12.4m revenue stream.

The cap table confirms that the proposed acquisition "constitutes a Change of Control" under the definition in the company's capitalization structure (sample_data_room/Northwind_Logistics/cap_table_summary.pdf.md).

### B. Board presentation misstatement vs. MSA reality

The Q1 2026 board deck, prepared for internal management discussion, states: "No customer-termination, change-of-control, or contract-portability items are flagged in this excerpt." (sample_data_room/Northwind_Logistics/board_deck_excerpt.pdf.md) The same deck calls the concentration profile "well-mitigated by contract duration" and says it presents no material near-term revenue risk. The Meridian MSA says the opposite: Meridian may terminate effective immediately on a change of control, and the contract tenor the deck relies on is exactly what Section 12.3(c) overrides. The board was given a misstatement of the customer risk.

### C. Harbor Foods termination for convenience

Harbor Foods Distribution represents $6,800,000 of ARR, or 16.5% of total revenue (sample_data_room/Northwind_Logistics/arr_schedule.xlsx.md). The Harbor MSA permits customer-initiated termination for convenience upon thirty (30) days' prior written notice (sample_data_room/Northwind_Logistics/msa_harbor_foods.pdf.md), with pro-rata refund of any prepaid fees.

While not triggered by change of control, this termination right represents material revenue volatility and should be monitored during integration.

### D. Cobalt Retail multi-year prepaid order and revenue-recognition risk

Cobalt Retail Group represents $9,600,000 of ARR under a 36-month prepaid arrangement totaling $28,800,000 in total contract value (sample_data_room/Northwind_Logistics/order_form_cobalt_retail.pdf.md). The full prepayment was collected in January 2025, creating significant cash inflow but also revenue-recognition exposure.

The ARR schedule's own recognition note, flagged for review, says the arrangement was recognized with a front-loaded Year-1 tranche rather than ratably over the 36-month service period, which overstates recognized revenue and ARR quality (sample_data_room/Northwind_Logistics/arr_schedule.xlsx.md). Buyer should confirm the ASC 606 treatment of this $28,800,000 commitment and that deferred revenue at 2026-03-31 reflects the unconsumed service period.

### E. Tidewater EU data-processing gap: unregistered sub-processor

Tidewater Distribution N.V., a Belgian customer representing $3,100,000 of ARR, is an EU entity subject to GDPR. The company has executed a Data Processing Addendum with Tidewater (sample_data_room/Northwind_Logistics/dpa_tidewater.pdf.md) covering EU personal data transfers.

However, the Sub-Processor Register documents a material compliance gap: TelemetryWorks Inc., a US-based analytics vendor, processes real-time driver location and telematics data for the Tidewater tenant but is not listed in the Tidewater DPA sub-processor annex. Critically, "No EU Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) are on file" for this transfer of EU personal data to the United States (sample_data_room/Northwind_Logistics/subprocessor_register.pdf.md).

The register records the gap as open and unresolved: no Article 28 data-processing agreement with TelemetryWorks, no transfer mechanism, and no notification to Tidewater under Section 5 of the DPA. Remediation items are all marked not started.

### F. Core IP ownership gap: contractor-built route-optimization engine

The company's route-optimization engine is described as a core differentiating feature of the platform, built by an independent contractor under Statement of Work 2024-RE-01 effective March 4, 2024.

The contractor agreement states: "This Agreement contains no provision assigning, transferring, or vesting in Company ownership of, or any work-made-for-hire designation with respect to, the intellectual property, source code, algorithms, or other work product created by Contractor in connection with the Services." (sample_data_room/Northwind_Logistics/contractor_agreement_route_engine.pdf.md)

By contrast, the company's standard Employee Proprietary Information and Inventions Assignment Agreement provides that employees "hereby irrevocably assigns" all right, title, and interest in work product to the company (sample_data_room/Northwind_Logistics/employment_ip_agreement.pdf.md).

The company therefore holds the route-optimization engine under a non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free licence from the contractor (Section 6.2), not as owner. The contractor retains the intellectual property in a feature the statement of work calls core to the platform.

## 3. Most Material Issue: Quantification and Deal Action

### Financial Impact

The Meridian Freight revenue cliff is the most material finding. The customer represents $12,400,000 of $41,200,000 total ARR (sample_data_room/Northwind_Logistics/arr_schedule.xlsx.md). The change-of-control termination right is unconditioned, automatic upon written notice, and effective immediately.

Under the ARR schedule, Meridian accounts for 30.1% of contracted annual revenue and is Northwind's flagship reference account.

Loss of the Meridian contract would result in a direct revenue loss of $12.4m per annum, representing 30.1% of total ARR.

### Recommended Deal Action

**Reprice the transaction at close by $12.4m via one or more of the following mechanisms:**
- Price reduction of $12.4m to be taken at closing
- Escrow of $12.4m held as security for Meridian revenue through the initial post-close period
- Special indemnity from seller covering revenue loss if Meridian exercises the termination right within sixty (60) days of close

**Contingent conditions:**
- Obtain written confirmation from Meridian Freight, prior to close, of its intent to continue the relationship post-close
- Alternatively, secure a waiver or amendment to Section 12.3(c) of the Meridian MSA from Meridian prior to close
- If neither confirmation nor amendment is obtained, the $12.4m reduction or escrow must be held at close to cover the anticipated loss

## 4. Lesser and Lower-Priority Issues

### Granite Manufacturing consent-based change-of-control clause

Granite Manufacturing Co. (10.9% of ARR, $4,500,000) holds a change-of-control clause in its MSA (sample_data_room/Northwind_Logistics/msa_granite_manufacturing.pdf.md). However, this clause is materially different from Meridian's automatic termination right.

Granite's provision requires consent from the non-assigning party before assignment, such consent not to be unreasonably withheld, conditioned, or delayed. Critically, a Change of Control shall not, by itself, give either Party any right to terminate this Agreement. This is a consent-based, benign change-of-control clause that protects Granite's relationship but does not create a termination right.

Granite does not present the same revenue-cliff risk as Meridian.

### Harbor Foods termination for convenience

As noted above, Harbor Foods (16.5% of ARR) retains a termination-for-convenience right. This is a general commercial risk applicable to most SaaS customers.

### Cobalt Retail revenue-recognition confirmation

The Cobalt $28,800,000 prepaid order warrants confirmation that revenue recognition is correct and that deferred revenue balances reconcile to the remaining service period.

### Tidewater EU SCCs and sub-processor remediation

The Tidewater sub-processor gap should be remediated prior to close by executing Standard Contractual Clauses or re-routing telemetry data processing within the EEA.

## 5. Confidence and Open Items

### High confidence

The Meridian MSA change-of-control termination right is documented and unambiguous. The provision is unilateral (customer-elected), unconditioned (triggered by change of control alone), and self-executing (effective immediately upon written notice). The revenue impact ($12,400,000 ARR) is quantified in the ARR schedule, the customer concentration (30.1%) is repeated in the board deck, and the cap table confirms that the transaction constitutes a Change of Control.

### Items requiring confirmation

1. **Meridian intent letter.** Obtain written confirmation that Meridian does not intend to exercise the termination right. This is critical.

2. **Board minutes.** Review board minutes to understand why the change-of-control risk was not flagged.

3. **Tidewater remediation feasibility.** Confirm with the DPO whether the sub-processor gap can be remediated before close.

4. **Contractor follow-up.** The Section 6.2 licence is worldwide and royalty-free but silent on duration and survival; obtain an assignment or a perpetual, irrevocable licence from the contractor before close.

The Meridian revenue cliff is the deal-defining issue. Pricing at $12.4m accounts for the material change-of-control risk.
